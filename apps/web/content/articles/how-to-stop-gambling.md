The usual advice is to decide to stop and then be strong. That is the wrong way round. Almost everyone who gambles too much has already decided to stop, many times, usually at about two in the morning. The decision is not the missing piece.

What works is less heroic and more practical: arrange your life so that gambling is slow, awkward and visible, and so that when the urge arrives there is something between it and your money. An urge is a wave. It builds, peaks and passes whether or not you act on it. Every barrier you put up buys time for it to pass.

So this guide is mostly about barriers, in the order to put them up. The first three sections can be done today, in under an hour.

## 1. Tell one person

Before anything technical. Choose someone you trust — a partner, a friend, a sibling — and tell them the truth, including the number if you know it.

It is the step people most want to skip, and it does the most. Gambling problems live on secrecy: the hidden account, the deleted history, the explanation kept ready. Once one person knows, the secret has stopped doing its work, and you have somebody to send a message to at the moment you would otherwise place a bet.

If there is truly nobody, a helpline adviser counts. That is what they are for.

## 2. Block yourself from the places you gamble

Do not rely on not logging in. Make it impossible to log in.

**Self-exclusion registers** bar you from every licensed site at once, for a period you choose and cannot undo.

- **Great Britain: GAMSTOP.** Free. You choose six months, one year or five years, and the exclusion cannot be removed during that time. It covers online gambling; betting shops, casinos and arcades run their own schemes.
- **Australia: BetStop.** Free, and overseen by the national communications regulator. A minimum of three months, up to a lifetime. Every licensed online and phone betting provider must close your accounts, refuse your bets and stop sending you marketing.
- **United States:** there is no single national register. Each state that has legal betting runs its own self-exclusion list through its gaming regulator, and each operator has its own as well. If you bet in more than one state, or across a state line, you need to do it in each. The national helpline can tell you where to find yours.
- **Elsewhere:** ask a helpline or your gambling regulator. Most countries with licensed online gambling have something.

Be aware of the gap. Self-exclusion covers *licensed* gambling companies in *that* place. It does not cover unlicensed offshore sites, and it generally does not cover products that are not legally classed as gambling, such as some trading and prediction-market apps. That is what the next step is for.

**Close the accounts, do not just abandon them.** Ask each company to close your account and to stop contacting you. Unsubscribe from the emails, turn off the notifications and delete the apps. Most relapses start with a “free bet” in an inbox.

## 3. Block the sites and the payments

**Blocking software** stops gambling sites and apps from loading on your phone, tablet and computer, whichever company runs them. BetBlocker is free, is run by a charity, works on all the main devices and needs no registration; you choose how long the block lasts. There are paid alternatives as well; in Britain one of them, Gamban, is offered free through the TalkBanStop partnership with GamCare and GAMSTOP. Install a blocker on every device you own, and choose a long period.

**A block on your card** stops the money. In the UK, most banks will switch off gambling payments on your debit card from the app or over the phone, and build in a delay of 48 or 72 hours before the block can be lifted again — long enough for an urge to pass. Elsewhere, ask your bank what it offers; many will lower limits or block categories of spending if you ask.

While you are there:

- remove saved cards from your browser and your phone's wallet;
- cancel or freeze credit cards, and turn off overdrafts if you can;
- lower your daily transfer and cash-withdrawal limits.

No single block is perfect. A determined person can get round any one of them. Three together turn “a few taps” into an hour's deliberate work, and an hour is usually enough.

## 4. Take money out of your own hands for a while

For the first few months, the less money you can reach quickly, the better.

- Have your pay go into an account that needs two people to move money, or have someone you trust hold the card for the account with savings in it.
- Carry only the cash you need for the day.
- Give someone else sight of your statements. Not as a punishment: as a net.

It can feel humiliating. It is also temporary, and it is one of the most effective things on this page.

## 5. Plan for the urge, because it will come

It will come at the times it always came: payday, a big match, a bad day, a boring evening, a drink. Knowing that is half of it.

- **Delay.** Tell yourself you can decide in an hour. Then do something that takes your hands and attention: a walk, a shower, a call.
- **Send the message.** To the person from step one. “I want to bet” is enough.
- **Fill the time.** Gambling took hours. An empty evening with a phone in it is the most dangerous thing in early recovery, so plan evenings.
- **Notice the thoughts.** “Just a small one”, “I've learned my lesson”, “I'm due a win” and “I'll win it back and then stop” are not plans. They are symptoms. We have written about [why the mind produces them](/articles/gamblers-fallacy-and-near-misses).
- **Never bet to recover losses.** The money is gone whatever you do next. The next bet is a new bet at the same bad price.

## 6. Talk to people who do this for a living

It is free and confidential, and you do not have to be sure you have a problem before you call.

- **United Kingdom:** the National Gambling Helpline, run by GamCare, on 0808 8020 133. Free, 24 hours a day, every day.
- **United States:** the National Problem Gambling Helpline on 1-800-MY-RESET (1-800-697-3738). Call, text or chat, at any hour; it connects you with help in your state.
- **Anywhere:** Gambling Therapy offers free online support in many languages, and Gamblers Anonymous runs meetings, in person and online, in many countries.

Links to all of these are on [our help page](/help). An adviser can also tell you what treatment is available where you live.

**Treatment works, with a caveat.** The best-studied treatment is cognitive behavioural therapy, which deals directly with the beliefs and habits that keep gambling going. A review of the trials found that it reduces gambling and its symptoms in the period straight after treatment. The same review was frank that the evidence was limited and that less is known about how well the gains last, which is one more reason to keep the barriers up afterwards. In England, the NHS runs specialist gambling clinics that you can refer yourself to. If you are also low, anxious or drinking more than you would like, tell a doctor: these travel together, and treating one helps the other.

## 7. Deal with the debts openly

Debt is what drives people back, because one big win looks like the only way out. It is not a way out. It is the way in.

Free debt-advice charities exist in most countries and will help you make a plan with what you actually owe. In the UK that is StepChange or National Debtline; in the US, a non-profit credit counsellor. Do not take out a new loan to clear gambling debts until you have talked to one, and do not let a family member quietly pay everything off. It feels like kindness and removes the reason to change.

## 8. If you slip

Many people who stop gambling do not manage it at the first attempt. A slip is information, not a verdict.

Ask what happened just before. Which barrier was missing? What time was it, what had the day been like, where did the money come from? Then put that barrier up, tell your person, and carry on. The worst thing a slip can do is convince you that nothing works. The second worst is persuading you to win back what it cost.

## The short version

- Deciding to stop is not the hard part. Arrange things so that gambling is slow, awkward and visible.
- Today: tell one person, self-exclude, install a blocker, block your card.
- Close accounts and kill the marketing. Most relapses start in the inbox.
- Take quick access to money away for a few months.
- Call a helpline. It is free, and they have heard it all before.
- A slip is a reason to add a barrier. It is never a reason to chase.
